Automation forgot about the physical world. We didn't.

The last decade of automation was built for webhooks between SaaS apps — stateless, fire-and-forget, taxed per task. That model breaks the moment a greenhouse, a vehicle or a lab instrument enters the loop. wbskt started from the other end: hold a live connection to everything, keep state in the platform, and charge for what actually runs.

01
The connection stays open

Polling is a workaround, not an architecture. If it matters enough to automate, it matters enough to hold a live connection.

02
State is the platform's job

Engineers should write logic, not checkpointing code. Runs pause, resume and survive restarts because the platform remembers.

03
Trust is granted, not assumed

Every integration is sandboxed with explicit, revocable permissions. Convenience never buys its way past isolation.

04
Price the work, not the count

Charging per task punishes exactly the systems automation is for. We meter compute and connections — nothing else.
