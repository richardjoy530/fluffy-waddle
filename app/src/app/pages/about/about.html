Automation skipped over the physical world.

The last decade of automation tooling was built to move data between SaaS apps: stateless, fire-and-forget, and billed by the task. That model starts to break the moment a greenhouse, a vehicle or a lab instrument is part of the loop — things that go offline, take minutes to respond, and cannot simply be retried from the beginning. wbskt was built from the other end: hold a live connection to everything, keep the state in the platform, and charge for the work that actually runs.

01
The connection stays open

Polling is a workaround for not having a connection. If something matters enough to automate, it matters enough to stay connected to.

02
State is the platform's job

Engineers should be writing the logic, not the code that saves its progress. Runs pause, resume and survive restarts because the platform remembers for them.

03
Trust is granted, not assumed

Every integration is sandboxed with permissions you grant explicitly and can revoke at any time. Convenience never buys its way past isolation.

04
Price the work, not the count

Charging per task penalizes exactly the systems that automation exists to serve. We meter connections and compute, and nothing else.
